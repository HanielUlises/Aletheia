# KD45 domains

Behaviour of the planner on the doxastic (belief) fragment of the local
benchmark suite. For the design of the product update and the seriality repair
see §2.2 of the [main README](../README.md); for the S5 timings see
[evaluation.md](evaluation.md).

Measured on the local suite, GCC 11.4, `-O3` with LTO, single core. Wall-clock
figures are the mean of 20 runs (10 for `depot3`) of the whole process and
include ≈1.5 ms of start-up and parsing; "search" is the planner's own
`elapsed_sec` counter. Seriality figures come from a probe build that counts
every call into `product_update_with_map` and `serial_core`; the probe is not
part of the shipped binary.

## 1. The instances

A task is doxastic when its `requirements` list carries `:KD45-frames`,
`:belief`, `:doxastic` or `:kd45`; the parser reports the decision on stderr and
the flag reaches the product update as `enforce_kd45` (`src/parser.cpp:263`).
Seven of the twenty-one instances in `benchmarks/` are KD45:

| instance | agents | atoms | actions | initial worlds | goal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `backdoor` | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | depth 1 |
| `whisper` | 3 | 2 | 1 | 2 | depth 1 |
| `sally-anne` | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | depth 1 |
| `depot1` | 3 | 29 | 315 | 1 | `at_c1_bay2 ∧ [chief] at_c1_bay1` |
| `depot2` | 3 | 29 | 315 | 1 | as `depot1`, plus `[rob1][chief] at_c1_bay1 ∧ [rob1] at_c1_bay2` |
| `depot3` | 3 | 29 | 315 | 1 | `at_c1_bay2 ∧ C_{chief,rob1,rob2} at_c1_bay2` |
| `depot5` | 3 | 29 | 315 | 2 | as `depot3` |

The four `depot*` instances share the `doxastic-depot` domain, whose `relocate`
action is a private ontic action — the false-belief generator — and whose
`broadcast` / `whisper` / `brief` actions are public, private and quasi-private
truthful announcements.

## 2. Results

All seven instances are solved. Every returned plan is accepted by an
independent validator (`plank validate`, checked against both builds present on
the machine), so this section reports behaviour, not correctness failures.

| instance | strategy (rule) | heuristic | plan | expanded | generated | search | wall | peak RSS |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `backdoor` | AO\* (`kd45-small`) | `ug` | 1 | 3 | 1 | 0.02 ms | 1.77 ms | 4.2 MB |
| `whisper` | AO\* (`kd45-small`) | `ug` | 1 | 3 | 1 | 0.02 ms | 1.54 ms | 4.2 MB |
| `sally-anne` | AO\* (`kd45-small`) | `ug` | 1 | 3 | 2 | 0.03 ms | 1.60 ms | 4.2 MB |
| `depot5` | AO\* (`sensing-small-designated`) | `ed` | 1 | 3 | 84 | 0.23 ms | 11.27 ms | 7.7 MB |
| `depot1` | AO\* (`sensing-small-designated`) | `ed` | 2 | 2,447 | 2,592 | 7.40 ms | 19.14 ms | 7.7 MB |
| `depot2` | AO\* (`sensing-small-designated`) | `ed` | 2 | 2,447 | 2,592 | 7.68 ms | 19.97 ms | 7.8 MB |
| `depot3` | AO\* (`sensing-small-designated`) | `ed` | 3 | 235,820 | 236,013 | 853.8 ms | 917.21 ms | 7.9 MB |

Two structural observations.

**The KD45 flag decides the strategy, not only the semantics.** Every doxastic
instance routes to AO\*: the small ones by the `kd45-small` rule, which exists
precisely because seriality repair can leave conditional structure in a domain
with no sensing events, the `depot*` family by `sensing-small-designated`. The
resulting plan trees are nevertheless degenerate — every node in all seven plans
has exactly one branch — so the doxastic instances pay for a conditional search
and return a linear plan.

**`depot1` and `depot2` are the same search.** The two differ only in the goal
(`depot2` adds two conjuncts, one of them nested), yet expansions, generated
nodes and the returned plan coincide to the digit. The plan is genuinely a
solution to the stronger goal — `plank validate` returns `true` on both — so
what the coincidence measures is the `ed` heuristic's insensitivity to the extra
conjuncts, not a goal-checking shortcut.

## 3. Seriality repair

`serial_core` (`src/product_update.cpp:119`) computes the greatest fixpoint of
"every agent's row, restricted to survivors, is non-empty" by repeated sweeps,
and the caller drops the worlds outside it. Its activity over a whole run:

| instance | KD45 product updates | sweeps | repairs | worlds deleted | `NonSerial` prunes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `backdoor` | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `whisper` | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `sally-anne` | 2 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `depot5` | 84 | 84 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `depot1` | 2,592 | 2,592 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `depot2` | 2,592 | 2,592 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `depot3` | 237,650 | 239,287 | 1,637 | 2,535 | 1,637 |

Three things follow.

1. **On six of seven instances the repair never fires.** The product update
   preserves seriality on every applicable action there, and the machinery costs
   one fixpoint sweep per update — 1.007 sweeps per call on `depot3`, exactly one
   elsewhere, i.e. the loop terminates on its first pass whenever nothing is
   deleted.

2. **When it fires, it is all-or-nothing.** On `depot3` the repair count and the
   `NonSerial` prune count are identical: 1,637 repairs, 1,637 prunes. Not once
   in a quarter-million updates did deleting worlds produce a smaller *usable*
   model — every deletion cascade reached the designated set and killed the
   successor outright. On this suite the repair is therefore not a repair but an
   applicability test: an action either leaves the frame serial or is discarded.

3. **The pruned actions form one class.** The 1,637 prunes fall on 19 distinct
   ground actions, and the histogram is dominated by announcements about the
   crate's true location:

   | action family | prunes each |
   |---|---:|
   | `brief_{rob1,rob2}_chief_c1_bay2_*`, `broadcast_{rob1,rob2}_c1_bay2` | 172 |
   | `brief_chief_{rob1,rob2}_c2_bay2_*`, `broadcast_chief_c2_bay2` | 35 |
   | `peek_chief_c1_bay2`, `inspect_chief_c1_bay2`, `relocate_chief_c1_bay2_*` | 4 |

   All share one shape: the addressee holds a belief the event contradicts
   (`[chief] at_c1_bay1` against an announcement of `at_c1_bay2`), no surviving
   event is compatible with that belief, and the chief's accessibility from the
   designated world comes out empty. `depot5` is the control: the same
   `brief_rob1_chief_c1_bay2_bay1` is applicable there and solves the instance in
   one step, because its two-world initial model leaves the chief *uncertain*
   rather than *wrong*.

## 4. Ablation: what seriality repair costs

Disabling the repair (treating the task as S5 while keeping the same task file)
isolates its effect:

| instance | repair on: plan / expanded / search | repair off: plan / expanded / search |
|---|---|---|
| `depot1` | 2 / 2,447 / 7.40 ms | 2 / 2,447 / 7.63 ms |
| `depot2` | 2 / 2,447 / 7.68 ms | 2 / 2,447 / 7.69 ms |
| `depot5` | 1 / 3 / 0.23 ms | 1 / 3 / 0.24 ms |
| `depot3` | 3 / 235,820 / 853.8 ms | **2 / 101 / 0.78 ms** |

Where the repair does not fire it costs nothing measurable — the differences on
`depot1`, `depot2` and `depot5` are below run-to-run noise, and the sweep itself
is `O(|W|·|Ag|)` word operations against a product update that is already
quadratic. (For `backdoor`, `whisper` and `sally-anne` the ablation is not
semantics-only: clearing the flag also flips the strategy rule to `s5-partial-obs`
and hence to GBFS. All three still solve with the same one-step plan.)

`depot3` is the whole story. The two-step plan
`relocate_rob1_c1_bay1_bay2 ; brief_rob1_chief_c1_bay2_bay1` — relocate the
crate, then tell the chief — is what the planner finds the moment seriality stops
being enforced: **2,335× fewer expansions and ~1,100× less search time** than the
three-step plan it returns under KD45, which instead walks the chief physically
to the crate (`go_chief_bay3_bay2 ; go_chief_bay2_bay1 ;
relocate_chief_c1_bay1_bay2`).

The ablation is not a fix, and the gap is not a bug on either side. Under KD45
the announcement is inapplicable exactly as §2.2 describes; with the repair
disabled the resulting model is non-serial at the designated world, where every
`[i]φ` — including the goal's common belief — is vacuously true. `plank validate`
accepts the two-step plan (`true`) on the same grounds. What the divergence
records is a semantic commitment: **Aletheia implements belief expansion plus
seriality repair, and therefore rejects belief-contradicting announcements
outright, where a validator that tolerates non-serial models accepts them.** Any
AGM-style revision added to the planner changes the numbers in this section
first.

## 5. Instrumentation gaps found while measuring

Two counters read zero on precisely the route every doxastic instance takes.

- `PlannerStats::pruned_non_serial` is only incremented by `record_prune` in the
  GBFS and EHC loops (`src/search.cpp:148`, `:552`, `:623`). AO\*'s expansion
  discards the `Outcome` without recording it (`src/search.cpp:296–297`), so on
  `depot3` the planner reports `nonserial=0` against 1,637 actual prunes. Since
  the KD45 rules route every doxastic task to AO\*, the counter is dead exactly
  where it was introduced to be read.
- `peak_state_bytes` is likewise never filled on the AO\* route; the GBFS runs
  report it (e.g. 2.87 MB on `gossip1`), the AO\* runs report 0.

Separately, `product_update_split` (`src/product_update.cpp:325`) drops a branch
whose designated set comes out empty without recording it — 30,868 branches on
`depot3`, 324 on `depot1`. This is not doxastic-specific (`muddy3` drops 21 of
its 42 branches this way), but it is silent, and on KD45 tasks it is the second
place where an outcome can disappear from a conditional plan.
